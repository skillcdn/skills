# The screen: a cut sheet a video can be made from

What the viewer sees under each sentence, and what the screen carries so that the voice need not: the cuts, what a screen can be and where it comes from, how the ear and the eye share the load, the text on screen, and what is left to the stage that makes the video. Read before phase 4.

## The cut sheet

One table in `script.md`, one row per cut. A cut is a stretch of the report under one picture; it begins where the picture changes, which is usually where a part and its paragraph begin, and holds one to four sentences.

| Column | Holds |
|---|---|
| Cut | Its number |
| Sentences | The numbers, from the sentence table, of the sentences said under it |
| Seconds | From and to, from the count made when the draft stood (each sentence's share of the spoken length), as an estimate a recorded voice replaces |
| Screen | What is shown, and its class from the table below |
| Text on screen | The title, the supers, the figures, the quote and its source, as final text |
| Facts | The fact ids the text on screen rests on |

The first cut is the anchor on camera, from the first word to the handover.

## What a screen can be

In the order they are chosen:

| Class | Used for | Holds to |
|---|---|---|
| The anchor on camera | The lead and the handover | A studio, with the opening picture on a screen beside the anchor and the title across the foot |
| A graphic drawn in code | A definition, a count, a quote, two sides set against each other, a route or a map, the dates that come next | The exact figures, the quote as printed with its speaker and its outlet, who counted and up to when. It builds a line at a time, with the voice |
| A generated general scene | What the thing looks like in general: a place, an object, an action anyone could film there | Labelled as generated in the text on screen, in the viewers' language ("AI-generated scene"). No real person, no mark or logo, no text the viewer must read, and never offered as the event itself |
| Footage or photographs the user holds the rights to | The event itself | Used where the user says they hold them, with the credit on screen |
| Pictures the story's subject published itself (an agency's or a company's own release) | The thing itself, where its terms allow | Used under the terms its publisher states, with the credit. The terms are read first and named in before air, and a graphic stands in where they are not at hand |
| Another outlet's photograph or footage | Nothing, as planned | A brief may list such images with their terms, which usually forbid reproduction. A cut that needs one names the rights holder whose permission it waits for, and the graphic that stands in until then |

A report can be made from the first three alone, and a cut sheet that leans on the last two is one the user cannot produce without someone else's word.

## The ear and the eye share the load

- What the ear cannot hold is on the screen at the moment it is said: the exact figure under the rounded one, the quote as printed under its plain telling, the weekday beside a date, a body's full name.
- The title across the foot says what happened and what is pending, in a line. It may carry the news the lead leaves for the end, so that the lead can stay with the viewer.
- The screen states nothing the script's facts do not hold, and never a second story the voice does not tell.
- A scene shows what its sentence says. A general scene chosen for mood under a sentence about a count is a missed graphic.
- Two sides are given the same kind of screen and the same weight.

## What the video stage decides

The cut sheet is taken over by whoever makes the video, a person or a skill of the pack that drives a video tool. This skill leaves to that stage, and names in its delivery:

- **The voice of the narrated stretch.** Most of an anchor report is the anchor's voice over pictures. How that voice is made, and that it matches the anchor on camera, is that stage's first decision and its first cost.
- **How a line is said.** The script is delivered as it is written. A line respelled for a generated voice is that stage's, by its own tool's page; for the pack's Higgsfield skills that is [pronunciation.md](/docs/higgsfield/pronunciation.md).
- **The length of a generated clip**, which that stage reads from its tool. A lead longer than one clip is cut at a sentence end, and the cut sheet lists the lead's sentences one by one so that it can be.
- **Text over the picture.** The pack's video skills draw every title, super, figure and label in code over the footage and ask no video model for text ([`docs/higgsfield/`](/docs/higgsfield/README.md)); the cut sheet's text column is written to be drawn that way.
- **The label on what is generated.** A generated anchor and generated scenes are delivered as generated, and the platform's label for AI-made content is the user's to set.
