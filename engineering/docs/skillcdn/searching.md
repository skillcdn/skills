# Searching a pack of documents

How a search over a pack behaves, and how to ask it so that it answers. The rules are SkillCDN's ([the words that match](https://github.com/skillcdn/skillcdn/blob/main/docs/specs/tools.md#the-words-that-match)); this page says what they mean for a run.

## What a search does

- `search_repo` matches words, and meaning where the deployment embeds the pack: the connection's instructions say which (`search_repo matches original words`, or words and meaning, in which case a question may also be asked in the agent's own words), and a result ends with a line on how it was ranked: `Ranked by words and meaning.`; the same with `<n> of <m> documents embedded so far` while the pack's embedding goes on, the rest found by their words alone; `Ranked by words alone this time` when the search by meaning did not answer; `Ranked by words alone: a query of numbers and names is matched as written.` for a query of identifiers (SkillCDN 0.5.1, checked 2026-11). By words, any word of the query may match and the results are ordered by how well, a word few documents hold deciding more than one every document repeats, with titles and descriptions weighing more than body text and a document's headings between the two, so that a code or a name quoted as a heading finds its section before a table that lists it among many; by meaning, the sections nearest the question, and a skill's name and description as one text, are fused into the same order. A skill stands only a little ahead of a document: its score counts 1.05 times a document's, enough to pass the documents up to three places ahead of it, so a document that matches better comes first (SkillCDN 0.5.1, checked 2026-11).
- A result that is the `README.md` of its folder is marked as the folder's overview: it is the map, which says a little of everything in the folder, and the document it names is the one to read.
- A result marked `(made from other documents: quote what it points to)` is a note or a glossary made from the pack (`derived` in its front-matter): it points to sections, and what is quoted is the section its block quote links to, read there, never the note (SkillCDN 0.5.0).
- The connection's instructions name the pack's manifest (`Manifest: read_repo_file SKILLCDN.md ...`), and `browse_repo` on a folder names the folder's own: its front-matter is where a pack keeps its metadata, what it is and, for a pack of documents, what it was made from and when, and its body the rules for reading it. Read it when the question is about the pack itself.
- A document result names the sections that matched, best first, each with its heading path, the `offset` and `limit` that read it with `read_repo_file`, and an excerpt of the passage. Over MCP a result names at most two sections and a page holds five results by default; `limit` raises it to twenty-five, and `nextCursor` continues.
- `path` scopes a search to a folder: the document set a map named. Scope first where a map puts the question in one set; a search over the whole pack ranks every set against every other.

## Words

- Search in the language the pack is written in, which the connection's instructions name (`Written in ko`). A question in another language is translated into the pack's words first.
- English words are matched by their stems: `invoices` finds `invoice`, `refunded` finds `refund`. Korean, Japanese and Chinese words are matched by pairs of characters, so an inflected word finds its stem and a particle written onto a word does not hide it; still, search for the stem (`연차`, `법인카드`) rather than the sentence.
- A number, a code, a path or a version is matched as written: `429`, `SKILL.md`, `2026.2`. A token joined by hyphens, slashes, dots or underscores (`open/closed/held`, `ops.intake.queue`, `TIDE_RATE_C2`) is a word whole and by each of its parts, except a part of digits alone, so a section number or a version stays one word. A name joined by capitals (`TideRateTable`) is found by the words it is joined of (`tide`, `rate`, `table`) where it names something, in a document's or a skill's name and title, in its path and in its headings; in body text and in a query it stays one word, so the name asked for as written still matches whole (SkillCDN 0.5.3, reading rules version 19, checked 2026-11). A query of at most four names and numbers (each with a digit, a capital after its first letter, or a word of kind such as `table`, `section`, `annex`) is ranked by its words alone, and the document whose heading begins with them stands first: ask for a clause or a table by its number or name as the pack writes it (SkillCDN 0.5.1, checked 2026-11).
- Use the pack's own words: the heading a map promised, the term the documents define. By words, a synonym the pack does not use finds nothing; where the connection says the pack is searched by meaning too, ask once more in your own words, since a section near in meaning is found without the pack's word, and a commit embedded a moment ago may still answer by words alone for a while.
- Fewer words find more: two or three words a reader would use, not the question as a sentence. Stop words (`the`, `how`, `is`) are dropped anyway.

## When a search finds nothing

In this order, one change at a time; a search that was scoped with `path` is first run again over the whole pack with the same words, which is not a change:

1. Another word for the thing, from the map or the description of the document that should hold it.
2. The stem or the base form, without the particle, the plural or the inflection.
3. The folder of the document set alone, with `path`, in case the whole pack buried it.
4. The outline of the document that should hold it: `browse_repo` with the file's path lists its headings; read the section whose heading fits.
5. Three searches and an outline that find nothing: the pack does not say it. The answer says so, and what was tried.

## Reading what was found

- Read the section the result named, with its offset and limit, before quoting; the excerpt is a glimpse, and a match can be in a heading or a table rather than the passage.
- A page lists the links in it after its text, by file, and where a link names a section (`#4.2.7`), that section with the offset and limit that read it: follow a reference by reading that section rather than the file. A document's outline says how many tables and code blocks it holds and where they are had as data (`Tables: 2 with 40 rows; as data: <where>`): a table a task needs whole is fetched from there by a program, never copied out of a tool result (SkillCDN 0.5.0).
- A section that starts with a heading and ends at the next heading may defer to its neighbour ("see below"): read the neighbour. A section at the start of a document is often the introduction; the detail is in the sections after it.
- A document's outline and the first page of its text name the originals it was made from (`Original:`, from its front-matter `source`), each with where its bytes are, and the pictures it links to with their words. A file that is not text, an original or a picture, is not read by `read_repo_file`: the answer says where the bytes are, at the git host, at the deployment's own route for a pack served from its directory, or on the machine for a directory the command reads (SkillCDN 0.5.1, checked 2026-11). A passage that describes a figure or transcribes a scan is the converting agent's words: an answer that hangs on it says so and names the original with where it is, in words for a page ("page 12 of the original"), since the hosts send an original as a download.
- A `Source` line names the commit a result came from; results of one run may come from different commits when the pack moves between calls, and while the pack's newest commit is still being indexed the line says so (`newest <commit> indexing`) and the result is read from the last indexed one. Quote the path and the heading path, which hold across commits; an offset holds only for the commit named with it, so a note kept for later carries none, and an answer given in the session that read it carries the offset and the limit with the commit, so that a reader opens the section in one call.
