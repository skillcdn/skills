# Minutes of a client meeting, in HTML and PDF, from notes

Notes go in: what the person who sat in the meeting wrote down or remembers, in any shape (bullet points, a chat message, a transcribed voice memo), plus the facts the organization already holds about the meeting (its date and place, who attended, the project's name, the previous minutes). Out come one minutes file (JSON: the header table and the sections), one self-contained HTML page, one A4 PDF printed from it, and a preview the agent has looked at, in the folder where the organization keeps this client's minutes. The minutes are a working document the client's own people use after the meeting, not a formality: decisions, questions and their answers, items to confirm, actions and the next meeting, each in one plain sentence, and nothing about why or how. The HTML opens anywhere and is what the next edit is checked against; the PDF is what gets sent. Nothing is generated by a model and nothing is spent; the only tool beyond the shell is a browser that prints to PDF.

## How the user is involved

- **Questions:** only for the notes, when the request has nothing to draw on, and once per organization for the house style, when neither the request nor the organization's records say: asked with the split checkpoint, in the user's language, as "Does your organization ban any characters or heading forms in external documents? The usual set is middle dots, em and en dashes, circled numbers and bracketed heads; one word keeps that set." The usual set is the default, and the answer is written into the organization's records (a `notes.md` in the brand folder, or the organization's handbook where it has one), so that the next run, for any client, finds it. Everything else (the meeting's facts, the language, the sections, the logos, the file name, the folder, what stays internal) is derived from the notes and the organization's records and stated at the first checkpoint.
- **Checkpoints:** the split (what goes into the minutes and what stays in the organization's own record) before the minutes file is written, and the preview after the render. Each is one message, as short as the split allows; "OK" continues.
- **Go-ahead:** when the user says to go ahead alone, both checkpoints are skipped; the delivery still lists what stayed internal and what to confirm, and the sending stays the user's word.
- **Sending and sharing:** the minutes leave the organization only when the user says they are final. Emailing them and posting them to a shared board or a project document are the user's explicit steps, never done on the way; the skill ends with the files in the folder and a note of what to send where.
- **Feedback rounds:** every note on a draft goes into the minutes file, and the whole page is rendered, looked at and delivered again as HTML and PDF. The HTML and the PDF are never edited by hand. A note on the look (a colored title, a logo too large, a section that reads as a label) is a note on the whole document: [review-notes.md](references/review-notes.md) keeps the notes reviewers give with the move that answers each.

## Inputs

| Input | Source |
|---|---|
| Notes | What the user brings back from the meeting. Asked only when the request has nothing to draw on. The notes stay as they are: nothing is written into them. |
| Meeting facts | Date, time, place, attendees with their titles, the project's name, the previous minutes: from the organization's records and the meeting's own entry; from the notes where the records are silent. An attendee the records do not name is not invented: the confirmed names with their titles, then "and others" in the minutes' language, when the room was fuller. The author is the organization's attendee who brought the notes. A weekday is computed from the date, never copied. |
| Language | The language the client reads, which is the notes' unless the records say otherwise. Everything in the file (the headings, the header labels, the columns, the kind) is in it, in the terms the organization's earlier minutes use; with none, the terms minutes-spec.md "Terms" gives. Sentences are plain declaratives in the register the earlier minutes use, with none the neutral written one (Korean 한다체), never the clipped note form. |
| Audience | Always external: the client and anyone between (an intermediary, another contractor). The house-style check is always on. |
| House style | The characters and heading forms the organization bans in external documents, from the request or the organization's records, asked once as above when nothing says; the usual set is the default. Left out of the minutes file when it is the usual set, which the helper then applies whole; written into the file's `style` only when the organization's set differs, in the same shape the deck skill (html-deck-from-outline) uses, so that an organization declares it once. |
| Logos | The client's logo and the organization's wordmark, from the organization's brand folder and the partner assets it already holds. The client's first. A logo the organization does not hold is not fetched: the header carries the organization's name in type instead. |

| Output names | Derived: `YYMMDD <project> <minutes>`, with the organization's word for minutes in the minutes' language (`260101 Example Project Minutes`), or the organization's own pattern where its earlier minutes show one. No meeting kind in the name, since the date tells meetings apart, and no client name, since the client reads it. In the minutes folder of this client's project folder; next to the notes when the organization has none. |

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Split the notes

Read the notes whole, then sort every statement into the sections it feeds: a decision (both sides agreed to it), a question and its answer, an item to confirm (left open, to be checked), an action (someone does something by some time), the next meeting, or internal (stays in the organization's own record). A statement may feed two: a decision that is also an action goes to both, an answer with an open end to the questions and to the items to confirm. A decision is written as what will be done, in one sentence, with the actor where one side does it. An answer the organization gave is softened to what was said and no more ("is expected to", not "will"). A figure the organization quoted in the room (a price, a size) crosses only with its caveat in the same sentence, under the question it answered or, when no noted question asked for it, under the items to confirm, and the checkpoint names it so that the user can strike it. An action with no time in the notes is an item to confirm, not an action with a time of the agent's. An action the other party does on its own (getting an internal approval, booking a room) is not listed; what both sides wait on is.

Apply the composition rules below while splitting. Show the split (checkpoint): the derived facts in one line (the title, the date, the place, the attendees, the file name and folder, the logos, the language), then the decisions in one line each, the questions and answers, the items to confirm, the actions with their owners and planned dates, the next meeting, and what stays internal, with the house-style question at the end when it is open. The user corrects anything; the corrected split is written, not argued.

### Phase 2: Write the minutes file

Write the minutes file as [minutes-spec.md](references/minutes-spec.md) defines it: `file`, `lang`, `kind`, `title`, `logos`, `style` only when the house style is not the usual set, `meta` (four rows: date and time, place, attendees, author; no distribution row and no subtitle), then `sections`, numbered in their headings and in this order: overview (two sentences at most, what the meeting went through), decisions, questions and answers, a reference table where the room was given several figures (unnumbered, "Reference."; one figure stays in its sentence), items to confirm, actions (a table of item, owner, planned), next meeting. A section with nothing in it is left out. Every string is final text in the minutes' language, within the house style from the first draft; the check in phase 3 is a net, not the editor. Nothing listed as internal at the checkpoint enters the file.

In the same run, write the organization's own record: what stayed internal, and what the meeting taught about the client that the records did not hold (the next meeting's date and place, a name, a title, an end time). It goes to the meeting's entry on the organization's board where there is one, else to the client's project page as a dated note, else to a Markdown file beside the minutes named `<file>-internal.md`; the notes themselves stay untouched. The delivery names where it went.

### Phase 3: Render and check

Run the helper, or write the HTML by hand from minutes-spec.md "Markup and style". The helper is `scripts/render.py` in this skill's directory: on disk in a copy or a plugin, and through SkillCDN read with `read_repo_file` at its path and saved in a tools folder of the run (the host's scratch folder), never in the client's folder, with `--out` pointing at the minutes folder. Run it with the Python that has Playwright:

```
python render.py <minutes.json> --out <folder> --png
```

It refuses a file that fails the house-style check and lists each offending string with its path in the file (`$.sections[2].items[0].body`) and the character's name; fix the minutes file and run again (`--check-only` runs the check alone; `--no-check` renders a draft for a look, and the delivery never uses it). It writes `<file>.html` with the stylesheet inline and the logos embedded (paths relative to the minutes file; a missing file is reported and renders no mark), prints `<file>.pdf` at A4 with the page number in the foot and says how many pages it has, and with `--png` saves `<file>-preview.png`, a full-page image of the HTML, beside them; `--sheet` tiles the PDF's pages into `<file>-pages.png` where PyMuPDF is installed. What the helper did in runs (the channel launch, a file name with spaces and another script, the page count) is in [tool-notes.md](references/tool-notes.md).

### Phase 4: Look and deliver

Look at the preview, then at the PDF page by page (the host's PDF reader, or the sheet) for the page breaks. Fix in the minutes file and render again until these hold:

- No table row is cut across pages and no heading sits alone at a page's foot.
- Nothing but black, grays and white in the document's own type, rules and cells; the logos keep their own colors, a line of text's height, the client's first.
- The header table has its four rows; every decision is one sentence, with its actor where one side does it; every action has an owner and a planned date or event; a figure from the room carries its caveat.
- Nothing internal, and nothing the notes and the records do not say.
- Every line broke at a space, never inside a word (Korean with `keep-all`); no owner or planned value in the actions table wraps, which the table's `widths` fix.

Show the preview, by its path or attached where the host attaches files (checkpoint).

Deliver the HTML and the PDF by path, the minutes file beside them, the page count, the house-style verdict, the list of what stayed internal and where the organization's record was written, and what the user should confirm before sending: an attendee's title or a missing name, the place's official name, a date the notes left open, a time or a figure that rests on the notes alone. Remove the run's own files from the client's folder (the preview image, a copy of the helper): they are working files, not something sent. Then stop: sending and sharing are the user's word, as separate steps.

## Composition rules

What reviewers send minutes back for. Apply them in phases 1 and 2, not after; [review-notes.md](references/review-notes.md) keeps the notes in their general form with the move that answers each.

- **Grayscale only.** Black type, gray rules, a light gray header cell; no colored title, side bar or accent, which read as machine-made. The logos are the one color on the page.
- **A section title is bold text over a thin rule**, not text beside a vertical bar.
- **Logos small, client first**, a hairline between them, on the top rule; the document kind ("Minutes") at the right in small gray type.
- **The header table carries four rows**: date and time, place (its official name), attendees (the confirmed names with titles, then "and others" when the room was fuller), author (a name, no date). No distribution row, no subtitle under the title.
- **Prose, not labels.** Numbered items, each a bold lead and one sentence; a table only for the actions and for figures given in the room; a table note ends in a noun or a verb stem, not a full sentence.
- **Open items are "items to confirm"** (Korean 확인사항), not "pending" or "under discussion": the heading says what the reader does with them.
- **Actions carry "planned"**, not "due": a date, "the next meeting", "on delivery".
- **Firm where it is a decision** (who owns what, what is delivered), **generic where it names the organization's own tooling** (the kind of thing it is, never a product's name and never "the tool").
- **Dates that carry no information stay out**: the date of a slide deck, a date beside the author.
- **One to three pages.** Minutes longer than that are cut, not shrunk: a section the room did not need, a caveat said twice.

## Hard rules

- Content comes from the notes and the organization's records. No decision, figure, name or promise the notes do not contain; where the notes are silent, the minutes are silent or say the item is to be confirmed.
- Nothing internal crosses: no contract amounts, no partners or terms of the organization's own contracts, no costs or staffing, no remark about the client, nothing said for the room's own ears. The split checkpoint lists what stayed out, and it is written into the organization's own record in the same run.
- The minutes pass the declared house-style check before they are delivered. A client's logo is used only when the organization already holds it as an asset.
- The HTML and the PDF are rendered from the minutes file, never edited by hand; every change goes through the minutes file, and both files are delivered every time.
- The preview and the PDF's pages are looked at before the delivery. A render the agent has not seen is not delivered.
- The minutes are sent and shared only on the user's word, as separate steps; the skill never emails, posts or publishes them.
- Nothing is generated by a model and nothing is spent.

## Terminology

- **Minutes file**: the JSON that holds the minutes, the header and the sections; the single source the HTML and the PDF are rendered from.
- **Split**: the sorting of the notes into decisions, questions and answers, items to confirm, actions, the next meeting, and internal.
- **Items to confirm**: what the meeting left open and someone checks; a section, never "pending".
- **Planned**: the column that says when an action is meant to happen: a date, the next meeting, delivery.
- **House style**: the characters and heading forms the organization bans in external documents, checked before the render; declared in the minutes file's `style` only when it differs from the usual set.
- **Usual set**: the default house style: middle dots in every form, the em and en dashes and the horizontal bar, circled numbers, and bracketed heads.
- **Internal record**: the organization's own note of the meeting (a board entry, a project page, a file beside the minutes), where its judgments and what stayed out of the minutes go.
- **Preview**: the full-page image of the rendered HTML, and the PDF's pages, looked at before the delivery and removed after it.
