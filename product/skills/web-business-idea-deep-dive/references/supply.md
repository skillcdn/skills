# Supply: the routes to the material the product needs

How the supply branch names what the product is made from, lists every route by which it could be had, and tables what each route yields, costs and risks, so that the review can say which combination a pilot can stand on. Read by the supply branch and before the review's supply section is written.

## Name the material

Before any route, write what the product is made from, by kind: documents, data, images, recordings, people's work. For each kind, how much the first version needs and how much it needs to keep going (a stock once, or a flow every week), since a route that fills a pilot can fail a product.

## List every route

Write down every route before judging any: the answer is usually a combination, which a list that stops at the first workable route never shows. The routes to consider, each asked of each kind of material:

- The buyers bring their own: what they upload, hold or enter.
- Public material, analysed: what is published is read and turned into something new (a description, a statistic, a model's output), and the material itself is not passed on.
- Open data: what a government or an institution publishes for reuse, under the terms it states.
- Made new: written, generated or commissioned for the product.
- Licensed or bought: from its owner, a publisher or a marketplace of datasets.
- A partner supplies it: an institution or a company under a contract.
- Contributors: people paid or rewarded to provide it.
- Collected from other services' pages.
- Bought as a business: a company or a catalogue that already holds it.

A route that does not apply to the idea is listed with one line saying why.

## The routes table

| Column | Holds |
|---|---|
| Route | Which of the above, and from whom |
| Yield | How much it gives of what the first version and the running product need, with the evidence |
| Legal risk | The row of the law section's risk table it meets, or "none found" |
| Cost | Money, time and people, as a band |
| Lasts | What would cut the route off: a change of terms, a contract's end, a source that stops publishing |
| Use | `use`, `use with the design of row <n>`, or `not`, with the reason |

Whether a route is lawful is the law branch's finding, written in the risk table by its article or its case; this table points at that row and decides nothing by itself. The branches run side by side, so a route this note lists that the law note did not cover goes back to the law branch as one more question before the section is written, and until it is answered its row says "not researched".

## The combination

The section ends with the routes that together reach what the pilot needs, what each contributes, and what the product would add later. A changed form of the idea rests only on routes whose `Use` is `use` or carries a design; where no combination reaches the pilot's need, the section says so, and that is a reason the idea as given is stopped.
