# Showing that a website and a filer are one company

A brand, a product name or a website is what the user knows; a legal name with a corp code is what files. They often differ in name, and one name is often shared. Take the first of these that holds, and write it in the row's `identity` as a sentence with its two addresses.

## The order

1. **The business registration number.** The site's footer, its terms of service or its privacy policy states the operator's number; the DART overview states the filer's. Equal numbers settle it.
2. **The filed homepage.** The overview's homepage field is the site, or a page of it. It settles it unless the field is a group's site that several subsidiaries share.
3. **Subsidiaries and brands.** The site links its subsidiaries, its overseas entity or its brands, and the filing's notes list the same names as subsidiaries or as the business.
4. **The site's own numbers.** A site that publishes revenue by year agrees with the filed figures for two years or more.
5. **The address.** The same street address in both. It is weak alone: a shared office building, a registered agent.

One of 1 or 2, or two of 3 to 5, is an identity. One of 3 to 5 alone is written as "one fact", and the name alone is nothing.

## What does not decide it

- **The representative.** The filing names the registered representative directors; the press names a founder or a chief executive. They can be different people in the same company, and the same name in two companies.
- **A site with no operator on it.** A recruiting site, a campaign page or an app's landing page may state no business information at all. Go to the terms of service of the product itself, then to 3 and 4.
- **A similar legal name.** DART holds dissolved companies, renamed ones and namesakes: read the founding day, the industry and the address in the overview before a name is believed.
