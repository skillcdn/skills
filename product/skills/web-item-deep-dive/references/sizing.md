# Sizing: how big the market is and when it buys

How the market branch counts who could buy and what they spend, keeps every assumption in a table, sizes the consumer and the business cases apart, and finds the weeks of the year when demand gathers. Read by the market branch and before the review's market section is written.

## The shape of a size

A size is a product of factors, and each factor is a row:

`base` (how many people or organizations could buy) × `share` (the part that buys) × `spend` (what one pays in a year)

- **Base** comes from official statistics: a population count, a register of businesses or institutions, a licence count. The public sources by country, with how each is read, are in the set's [sources.md](/docs/market-research/sources.md).
- **Spend** comes from an official survey of spending, an industry's reported sales divided by its buyers, or the competitors' price bands. A band stays a band: the size is then a range.
- **Share** is the factor most often invented. It is taken from a measured figure (a survey, a competitor's reported users over the base) or it is an assumption, graded `estimate` and given as a range. A share with no source and no range is not multiplied in. Where nothing measured is found, a new entrant's share is taken at 1, 3 and 5 percent, working values marked as the assumption they are.

## The assumptions table

Every factor of every size is a row; a size whose factors are not all in the table is not written.

| Column | Holds |
|---|---|
| Factor | What it counts, in plain words |
| Value | The figure or the range, with its unit and its period |
| Grade | `official`, `filing`, `announced`, `estimate`, `press`, `measured` or `computed` |
| Source | The address of the page the sentence was read on, or "assumption" |
| Note | The limit of the source: an old year, a wider category than the item's, a sample |

## Three sizes, kept apart

1. **The whole market:** everyone who has the problem, at what they spend on it today, substitutes included.
2. **The base case:** the part the item as given can reach in the pilot's country through the channel it would sell in, at the share's range. Low, middle and high are three rows, not one number.
3. **The business case:** organizations that could pay for many users at once (how many there are, what a contract is worth), sized by itself whenever a business or an institution could be the payer.

The consumer and the business sizes are alternatives of who pays and are never summed. A size computed here is graded `computed`, with its formula beside it.

## The calendar

Demand for most items gathers at a few points of the year. Find them, since they date the pilot:

- What triggers the purchase: a season, a deadline, an enrolment, an exam, a fiscal year, a holiday. The official schedule that fixes it is the first source.
- Search interest by month for the buyer's words, graded `measured`, over at least two years so that one year's event is not read as a season.
- The start dates of competitors' ads, where they cluster.

Write the result as twelve months in a row, the peaks marked, with how many times a year demand gathers and for how long. The pilot's window is then stated from it: the peak it aims at, and how many weeks before it the pilot must be selling.

## What the section holds

The market section of the review, in this order: who buys and who pays, in two sentences; the three sizes, each a sentence with its range; the assumptions table; the calendar row with the pilot's window; and what could not be sized, with what was looked for.
