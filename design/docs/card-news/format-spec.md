# The format spec

The format spec is what a card-news format looks like, written down so that a new post can be built in the same shape: the canvas, the ways the format builds each kind of post, each slide type with its anatomy, the elements that ride on slides, the text density, the register, the caption conventions, what the format never does, and the nearest fonts and colors. The study skill measures it from a reference and writes it as a JSON file (`format-spec.json`) beside a short notes page (`style-notes.md`); the design variations write a designed one, from a measured spec and the look the user picked; the build skill reads either before it plans a single slide. Where this page or the build says the account, it means the format the spec describes. The example JSON the study skill ships is the shape; this page says what each field means, and a field an account needs and the shape lacks is added and named in the study's delivery. Numbers in this page are illustrative; the spec carries the measured values, and marks as an estimate anything read by eye rather than counted. A position in an anatomy is a block's edges as percentages of the canvas (top and bottom always, left and right where the block does not span the margins), because the build places every block by them and its review compares them on the PNG.

## Where a spec comes from

Four routes, by what the user wants of a reference; the build reads the spec of each the same way, slide type by slide type.

| The user wants | Route | The spec |
|---|---|---|
| Posts like a reference account's, or like one reference post | The study measures the account's visible posts, or the post with the account's visible posts of its kind | Measured |
| The reference with a slight change of direction (another palette, a serif headline, less text) | The study measures the reference; the design variations make four looks within one or two axes of it; the user picks one | Designed from the measured spec |
| A format of their own, from references they have not named (a new account, a magazine with no reference) | The study reads three to five accounts of the genre into the genre's common form; the design variations make four looks from it, one near and one far; the user picks one | Designed from the genre's spec |
| Something new from a reference: what it does, and none of how it looks | The study measures the reference; the design variations make four looks, each two or three axes from it and keeping what it does; the user picks one | Designed from the measured spec |

The study runs first, then the research where there is no brief yet (it reads the spec's categories, canvas and frames), then on a designed route the design variations (their looks carry the brief's words and photos), then the build; each stops at its own checkpoints, and the build, given a request with no spec, offers the routes and runs the chosen one before its plan. The variations' side of the seam (the unit, the start, the crowd, the distance, the handoff) is in the variation set's [handoff.md](/docs/variation/handoff.md) "A card news format".

A designed spec is the start spec with the pick written into its fields: the palette into `colors`; the type into `fonts` (for each face the look used, the nearest free web font of its class with where to load it, as the study names fonts) and into each block's size and weight; the composition into the edges of `cover` and of the types the look showed, and into every type's margins where the move is one of margins or of a side; the imagery into each anatomy's `photo`; the shape and surface into the boxes, rules and tags of the anatomies and of `elements`; the density and the register stay the start's, except the characters per line of a block whose width the look changed, counted on its render. What the pick did not move keeps the start's value, with its `estimate` and its `seen_in`; a type the look did not show takes the moved tokens and keeps its anatomy, except on the route to something new, where it takes the look's composition too (its margins, the side and the edges of its text block), so that nothing keeps the reference's layout; the handoff names each such type. The pick's values are chosen, not measured, and carry no `estimate`; a `never` the pick contradicts is dropped and named in the handoff. A designed spec leaves out `cadence` and `posts_read`, which are the reference's and not the user's, and takes `categories` from the user's words where they give them, else keeps the start's.

## Fields

| Field | Holds | Obtained from |
|---|---|---|
| `source` | Where the spec came from: `measured`, with what was read (`account`, `post` or `genre`); or `designed`, with the start spec's path, the route (`slight`, `genre` or `new`), the axes the pick moved, the look picked, the variation run's folder and the day. | The study writes `measured`; the variations' handoff writes `designed`. |
| `account` | The handle studied, the day it was read, how many posts were visible without signing in and how many were read; for a genre, each account read with its post; for a designed spec, the format's name (the user's account or magazine where they gave one), the references being in `source`. | The profile. Stays in the user's spec; never in a skill's files. |
| `categories` | The kinds of story the account posts, each with its share of the visible posts and one example line (a caption's first line, quoted to identify the post). | The profile grid and the post objects of the visible posts. |
| `cadence` | Posts per day or per week and the hours they go out, counted from the visible posts' times, pinned posts left out. | The post objects. |
| `canvas` | The ratio of a slide measured on the post pages, the platform's documented width and supported ratios with the day they were looked up, the size that follows (width over ratio), the slides per post as a range with a typical count, and `other_ratios`, one entry per other ratio some kinds use, with its size, `for` (those kinds) and `seen_in`. | The post pages; the platform's help pages. |
| `kinds` | Each kind of post the account makes (a story, an information or visit post, a list, an event, a report on broadcast or captured material, a single image, a video), keyed by a short name, with the builds it uses for it: each build's `slides` as a range, `text_on` (sentences on photos, short boxed lines, the caption alone, the material's own captions with the account's text on the cover only), `typical_order` of slide types, `order` as `fixed` or `varies` between posts of the build, and `seen_in`. | The posts read, grouped by kind and build. |
| `cover` | The anatomy of the first slide: photo treatment, the label (its kind of words, color, weight, italic or not, position), the headline (lines, characters per line, weight, color, style), the account's `mark` where the cover carries its logo (its place and size; the mark itself never enters a build), and `variants` for covers that differ (a list cover, a sponsored cover, a cover with a source card), each with `for`, the kinds or builds it goes with, so that the build picks the variant by the post's kind. The cover is described here and not in `slide_types`. The label's words are the account's series name and never enter a build; its look does. | Two or more covers, side by side. |
| `slide_types` | One entry per slide type the account uses other than the cover, each with a `name`, the `anatomy`, `carries` (the content it carries), `seen_in` and `tentative` when seen in one post only: a story slide (photo, one-line sub, three lines of body), a photo-only slide, a collage, a lead slide, a poster slide, an information slide, a frame stack. The anatomy's `photo` says how the type treats its photo (cropped full-bleed; kept whole with its marks; two stacked or side by side; flat color), and each text block has its edges, size, weight and color. A type that keeps frames whole also has `frames_per_slide`, `frame_edges` (each frame's top and bottom as percentages), `marks_kept` (a broadcaster's logo, labels, subtitles), `background`, `own_text` (`none` where the account writes nothing on it) and `material_text` (what the material's own text is). These are the only layouts the build composes from. | Every slide of the posts read, grouped by what repeats. |
| `elements` | What rides on slides of other types, each with its anatomy, the slide types it rides on, how often, and the content it carries: a serif note, an inset (a product's package, a poster, an archive photo as a rectangle on the photo), a talk box (a white box of one or two short lines), a source card, a source tag. | The `other` column of the slide log. |
| `density` | Per slide type and element that carries text of the account's, and for the cover's headline: lines, characters per line, sentences, characters per sentence, sub characters, where detail goes (the caption), one fact per sentence or not. Counts (lines, sentences) as the most common value and the range; lengths (characters) as the median, the middle half and the range. A type whose `own_text` is `none` has no entry, and the build writes nothing on it. | Counted on the slides, in the account's script; the material's own text is not counted. |
| `register` | How the slides, each element that carries text of the account's (keyed by the element's name) and the caption are each written, as patterns with what sounds wrong beside what sounds right: headline style, sub style, body endings, the voice, the phrasings that would not appear. Named in the devices of the account's language. Where posts of one kind differ, both with their counts and the content each went with, the majority as the default. | The slides and the captions read side by side; described in English, the account's own sentences not copied. |
| `caption` | The order of the caption's parts (facts with a date and a source, context, a closing line, the credit line, mentions, an information block for an event), the credit line's exact pattern, the information block's pattern, and whether hashtags appear. | Three or more captions. |
| `variants_by_content` | Which slide type or element the account uses for which kind of content (a list, a date, a quote, a number, a mood, a reaction, the thing a sentence names), so that the build chooses by content rather than by habit. | Where each variant appeared and what it carried. |
| `never` | Statements of what the account does not do, each naming the kind of post it holds for and how many of that kind were read: a call to action on a story post, numbers as overlays, text on every slide. Only from a kind read two or more times. | Absence across the posts read. |
| `fonts`, `colors` | The nearest free web fonts for the display, the serif and the label faces, with where to load them and the observed weights and width, each marked `guess` when identified by letterforms; the label color, the text color, the background a frame or a flat slide sits on, the gradient's darkness and the dim overlay as hex or rgba, each marked `estimate`. | The slides at zoom. |
| `posts_read` | The post ids read slide by slide with their kinds, builds and slide counts, so that a later run does not re-read them and a research knows what the account has already covered. | The run. |

## How the build reads it

The build takes `canvas.size`, or the size of the `other_ratios` entry whose `for` names the post's kind, and the build of `kinds` that fits the post (its `text_on`, `typical_order` and `order`) as its frame and its sequence, `density` and `register` (`headline`, `sub`, `body`, one key per element by its name, `caption`) as the checks every line passes, `cover` (in the variant whose `for` names the post's kind or build, else the plain cover, with the user's own mark at `cover.mark` where they give one and nothing there otherwise), `slide_types` and `elements` with `variants_by_content` as the only layouts it composes from, each at its measured anatomy, `never` as its limits (a type the spec maps to a content wins over a `never` for that content only), `caption.parts` and `caption.credit_line_pattern` as the template of its caption, and `fonts` and `colors` as its CSS tokens. The build has no layouts of its own: a beat the spec has no type for takes the nearest type the account has, said at the plan checkpoint, never a type the account does not use. A value marked `estimate` may be adjusted where a render shows it unreadable, and the build says so. A measurement missing from the spec is read from the study's slide log where it is beside the spec, or beside the start spec a designed one names in `source`, else the block is set where the type leaves room for it, centered in that space, and said so at the plan checkpoint.
