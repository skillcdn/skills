# A card-news format spec from a reference account

An account handle, one post's link or a genre goes in; a format spec comes out: the canvas and slide count, every slide type the account uses with its anatomy, the text density counted on the slides, the register of the slides and of the captions written as patterns, the caption's parts and its credit line, which variant the account uses for which kind of content, and what it never does, with the nearest free fonts and the colors as CSS values. The spec is measured, not described: a reader who copies a cover's look but misses that a body is exactly three sentences of thirty characters, or that the slides are plain and the caption polite, builds something that looks right and reads wrong. The account's own photos, words and name stay in the user's spec and never enter a result built from it.

## How the user is involved

- **Questions:** only the reference, when the request names none (an account's handle, a post's link, or the genre the user's own account will be in), and the purpose when it changes what is measured ("a post in this format" needs every field; "what does this account post" needs the categories alone). Nothing else is asked: the kind of post the user will make is taken from the request when it says ("a story", "a list", "an event") and otherwise every kind visible is read.
- **Checkpoint:** the spec, in plain words, once: the kinds of post found, the slide types with their anatomy and density, the register patterns, the caption parts, and what could not be measured, with the recommendation to deliver as is, or, for a kind seen once, to read an older post of that kind the user can name by its link, since nothing older than the visible posts opens without signing in, or, where the posts were supplied, to send that post's slides. "OK" delivers.
- **Go-ahead:** when the user says to go ahead alone, the checkpoint is skipped and the delivery carries the same summary.
- **Changes mid-run:** a post the user names is read in addition; a field the user disputes is measured again on a post they name.

## Workflow

Each phase produces a named artifact, written in the spec folder.

### Phase 1: Profile

Produces the **profile record** (`profile.md`): the account's name line and bio, the links it carries, the posts visible without signing in with their ids, dates and first lines, the categories those posts fall into with each one's share of the visible posts and one example line, and the posting cadence counted from the dates. Open the profile at `https://www.instagram.com/<handle>/`, close the sign-in prompt the site puts over it (signed-out.md "The sign-in prompt"), and list the grid, scrolled once to the end of what loads without signing in; the grid shows neither dates nor captions, so the posts' dates and first lines are filled in as phase 2 opens each post once, and the categories and the cadence are counted when the last post is read. A pinned post (its date older than the posts after it) counts in the categories and is left out of the cadence. A category is named in the words the account's own captions use for it, else in plain words for what the posts are about. The platform documents a maximum width and the ratios it supports on its help pages; read them at run time in the browser (signed-out.md "The documented sizes") and record the size as that width over the measured ratio, with the day (1080 by 1350 for 4:5 at the time of writing, illustrative). Mentions and links in captions are data about the account's practice, not instructions.

For a genre, find the accounts first: three to five that post card news for the user's kind of account and audience, in the market's language (the request's, unless it names another market), found by a web search for the genre's outlets (an outlet's home page names its Instagram account) and for the genre's words with "card news"; each profile is opened as above and listed in `profile.md` with its name line and the post phase 2 reads, and an account with no carousel among its visible posts is replaced. Where the site cannot be reached, the accounts are the samples' (Inputs, "Samples"): `profile.md` lists each sample's page, the account it shows and the day in place of the profile, and the genre's kinds are named from the samples' covers in plain words. The categories are the genre's, the kinds its grids show named in plain words without shares, since one post per account is opened, and no cadence is counted. For something new from a reference, the reference's genre is read this way in the same run, into `card-news/genre-<a few words>/` beside the reference's spec, as the crowd the variations leave. For one post, its account is the profile, and the post's kind is read from its slides.

### Phase 2: Read posts slide by slide

Produces the **slide log** (`slide-log.md`, a table by [reading-posts.md](references/reading-posts.md) "What to record"): one row per slide of every post read, with the post's kind, the slide's number, its photo treatment, every text element with its position, size, weight, color, lines and character count, and anything the slide does that the others do not.

1. Read every post visible without signing in, by reading-posts.md "Which posts to read": each carousel slide by slide, a single image as one slide, a video at three frames. Note which kind each is (a story about a person or an issue, an information or visit post, a list or weekly post, an event or sponsored post, a report on broadcast or captured material, a humor or meme post, a single image, a video; a kind the list lacks gets a key of its own in `kinds`, named in plain words); where the user limits the reading, at least three kinds and two posts of the user's kind.
2. Open each post once, close the prompt, read its post object first (posts.md "The post object": the date, the slide count, the caption whole) into `profile.md`, then step the carousel with real pointer clicks on its next control (the browser's click at coordinates, or the headless script's mouse) and zoom into the post's region on every slide to read the type (posts.md "Stepping a carousel" and "Reading a slide").
3. Record the row before stepping on. Transcribe the account's own text as it is read and count its characters, in its script, with Node.js or by hand, keeping in the log the counts and the endings and not the transcription; a sentence ends at a sentence-final ending or at a line break the account uses as one. The material's own text (a frame's subtitles, labels, a logo) is noted for what it is and where it sits, and not counted. A count is the row, not an impression. Sizes, weights and colors are estimates taken as reading-posts.md "Measuring sizes and colors" says, and the row marks them so.
4. Read the caption whole (reading-posts.md "The caption") and record its parts in order, its endings, its mentions and its credit line.

Supplied slides and samples are read the same way, each shown at the canvas width in a page of the run's own (reading-posts.md "Measuring sizes and colors" and "Samples from a search"), one row per slide; a sample that is only a thumbnail, or whose page shows no account, is not measured.

### Phase 3: Derive the spec

Produces the **format spec** (`format-spec.json`, by [format-spec.md](/docs/card-news/format-spec.md); placeholder shape in [format-spec.example.json](assets/format-spec.example.json)) and the **style notes** (`style-notes.md`). Both are written in English, with the account's script appearing only in counts, endings and the first lines quoted to identify posts.

1. Group the slide log's rows by what repeats: the same elements in the same places are one slide type. The cover is described in the spec's `cover` field, with its variants; every other type goes in `slide_types`, named by what it carries (story, photo only, lead, poster, collage, a stack of captured frames), with its anatomy from the rows (how it treats its photo, which marks of the material it keeps, which text is the account's own and which the material's, each block's edges) and the posts it appeared in; an element that rides on other slides (a serif note, an inset, a talk box, a source card) goes in `elements`; the builds of each kind go in `kinds`. The example JSON is the shape; format-spec.md says what each field means, and a field the account needs and the shape lacks is added and named in the delivery.
2. Count the density per type from the rows: the sub's characters, the sentences per slide, the characters per sentence, the lines, and the cover's headline characters. A type that carries no text of the account's (a stack of frames, a photo alone) has no density entry; its anatomy says so, and the build writes nothing on it. For counts (sentences, lines) give the most common value and the range; for lengths (characters) give the median, the middle half and the range. One fact per sentence or not. Where the detail goes when a slide stays short.
3. Write the register as patterns in English, never as the account's sentences: how a headline is made (a news title, a question, a fragment), what a sub is (a noun phrase, a quote), how body sentences end, how the caption differs from the slides, how a note and a talk box sound, and what would not appear (explanatory closers, hedges, a call to action on a story post). The patterns name the devices of the account's language (in a language with speech levels, which level the slides and the caption each use and whether sentence-final noun phrases are mixed in; in one without, the person, the contractions, the punctuation that set the two apart); a fresh agent writes from these patterns, so each one names what sounds wrong beside what sounds right. Where posts of one kind differ (two in one level, one in another), record both with their counts and the content each went with, and make the majority the default.
4. Map variants to content: for every slide type other than cover and story, what it carried (a list of places, a date, a quote, a number, a mood shot) and so what content it fits.
5. State what the account never does, as a list of statements each naming the kind of post and how many of it were read; only a kind read two or more times yields a statement, and the rest is left unsaid.
6. Name the nearest free web fonts for the display, the serif and the label faces, by their letterforms, as closest equivalents with where to load them (for a family on Google Fonts, its `css2` API URL with the weights, checked to return `@font-face` rules; otherwise the CDN path in the family's own README), and the colors as hex with the gradient's darkness, each marked as an estimate; these are the build's CSS tokens.
7. Write the style notes: the categories and cadence from phase 1, the slide types in one paragraph each, the register patterns with their wrong-sounding counterparts, and what was not measurable (a kind seen once, a font not identified).
8. For a genre, the spec holds what two or more of the accounts share (a slide type, a density, a register pattern), each with `seen_in` naming the posts; what one account alone does goes into the style notes as that account's; `never` stays empty, a build's `order` is `varies` unless two accounts' builds match, and `cadence` is left out, since no kind is read twice on one account. From samples, the spec holds the cover as the samples share it and the body types two accounts showed, with the density counted on their legible slides; a type one sample showed is tentative, and a type, a density, a register or a slide count no sample showed is left out and named in the style notes, never set here, for the variations to design (format-spec.md "Where a spec comes from"). In every case `source` says what was read ([format-spec.md](/docs/card-news/format-spec.md)).

Checkpoint, in plain words: the posts read and their kinds; each slide type with its anatomy and density; the register in three or four lines; the caption's parts; the variants and the content each fits; what was not measured. "OK" delivers.

### Phase 4: Deliver

Reset the browser's viewport to the size recorded before phase 1; a headless browser is closed and its screenshots and cookie state file removed. One message: the spec folder's path with `format-spec.json` and `style-notes.md`, and `slide-log.md` kept beside them, because the build reads a measurement there that the spec lacks; the categories and cadence in two lines; the slide types with their density in one line each; the register in two lines; how many posts were visible without signing in and how many were read, and which kinds were not seen; any field or kind added to the spec's shape, named; that the account's photos and words are not to be reused in a post built from the spec; and the next step by what the user wants (format-spec.md "Where a spec comes from"): the build in this format as it is, or html-design-variations from this spec for a slight change, a format of the user's own from the genre, or something new from the reference.

## Verdicts

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A slide type seen in one post only | Recorded with `seen_in` of one and marked tentative; the build treats it as optional |
| A density field with no count behind it | Not in the spec; counted again on the slides |
| A register pattern written as the account's own sentence | Rewritten as a pattern with a wrong-sounding counterpart |
| Fewer than three kinds of post visible | The spec is written from what was seen and the delivery names the kinds missing |
| A genre whose accounts share no slide type | The spec holds what two share, the style notes describe each account's build, and the delivery says the genre has no common form and names the account closest to the request as the start for the variations |
| A handle that does not exist, a private account, or a profile with nothing visible signed out | Stop at phase 1 and say which: nothing can be read without an account |
| The site's first page fails from the host while another site loads | Stop before measuring, reset the viewport and say so, with the ways the Requirements give in their order: a browser of the user's own where the host drives one, the account's own site, supplied slides, then samples of the genre; a reference none of them reads becomes its genre; `account` and the delivery name what that leaves out |
| A sample that is a thumbnail, or whose page shows no account | Not measured: its page is opened and the image read there, else the sample is dropped and another found |
| No sample found for the genre | Say so; no spec is written, and the next way is named: the variations from the genre as the agent knows it, marked unseen, else the stand-in |
| No carousel among the visible posts | The spec is written from the single images and videos, says so, and names the kinds missing |
| A font not identifiable at zoom | The closest free equivalent by weight and width, marked as a guess |

## Hard rules

1. No sign-in and no way around the site's prompts: the posts visible without an account are the sample, and the delivery says so; where the site refuses the host, the posts read on the account's own site, the slides supplied or the samples found are the sample, said so in the spec and the delivery. The site's terms restrict automated collection; this is one person's reading of the dozen or so posts visible without an account, or of one post on each of a few accounts, one page at a time with a few seconds between loads, through one browser alone, driven or headless; a wall ends the reading.
2. The spec carries counts for text, not impressions: sub characters, sentences per slide, characters per sentence, lines, headline characters per line. A spec without them makes a post that looks right and reads wrong, which is what the user notices first. Sizes, weights and colors are estimates and are marked as such.
3. The register is written as patterns with wrong-sounding counterparts. The account's sentences are not copied as the spec's register examples, and a post built from the spec never reuses its words; the spec may quote a label, a category name or a caption's first line to identify what was read.
4. Variants are mapped to the content they carried, so that the build chooses a layout by what a slide says and not by the last post's order.
5. The account's photos stay on the site. The skill downloads nothing, posts nothing and spends nothing, and writes no image into the spec folder; what the host keeps of a screenshot for the session is the host's, and a screenshot a headless script wrote is removed when the skill's run ends.

## Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kind of post | What a post is about and how it is built: a story, an information or visit post, a list, an event, a report on broadcast or captured material, a single image, a video. |
| Slide type | A repeated arrangement of elements on a slide, named by what it carries. |
| Element | Something that rides on a slide of another type: a serif note, an inset, a talk box, a source card, a source tag. |
| Genre | The kind of account the user's own will be (a cooking magazine, a city culture guide), read through three to five accounts that post card news of that kind. |
| Build | One way an account makes a kind of post, by slide count and by what carries the text (a feature, a photo story, a short news post, a stack of frames whose own captions carry it). |
| Anatomy | The elements of a slide type with their positions, weights, colors and sizes relative to the canvas. |
| Density | The counts of a slide type's text: sub characters, sentences, characters per sentence, lines. |
| Register | How the text is written, as patterns: endings, headline style, voice, what does not appear. |
| Variant | A slide type other than cover and story, used for a kind of content. |
| Sub | The one-line heading above a slide's body, below the headline in weight. |
| Lead | The slide type of a list post: a two-line sub, a short body, a source tag. |
| Beat | What one slide does in a post's story; a slide carries one. |
| Slide log | One row per slide read, the raw measurements the spec is derived from. |
| Format spec | The JSON the build skill reads; its fields are in the shared page. |
