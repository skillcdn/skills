# Reviewer notes: what a draft is sent back for

The composition rules and the design floor in SKILL.md came out of review rounds on real decks. This page keeps the notes a reviewer gives, in their general form, with the move that answers each, so that the next agent recognizes the note on its own draft and makes the move before the reviewer has to. Read it when a draft comes back with notes, and before planning a deck for a reviewer who has sent one back before. Nothing here names a deck, a client or a product; a note that names one slide usually names a default the whole deck repeats, and the move fixes the default.

## The cover

| The note | The move |
|---|---|
| The subtitle is a long explanatory sentence. | One tagline under ten words; the cover is the title, the tagline and the logos, matched to the organization's finished deck. |
| The tagline should carry the technical keywords the audience cares about. | The tagline names the technical substance of the work (the task, the technology, the result) in the audience's own terms. |
| The client's logo is missing from the cover; it always comes first. | `client_logo` on the cover, a thin rule, then the presenter's logo. |

## Pages

| The note | The move |
|---|---|
| Subtitles on every page. | A subtitle only where the slide needs its one sentence; most slides have none. |
| A note at the foot of every page, and a summary banner on most. | Both removed. A caveat is said in the meeting; a quality bar becomes one bold sentence on the scope slide. |
| The product's logo in the slide header is out of place. | Removed from the header. A product's mark belongs in its brand panel. |
| The slide title should be plain. | A title that names the slide's content ("Items to confirm"), not the speaker's stance toward the room. |

## Structure

| The note | The move |
|---|---|
| The layout is predictable and icons are overused. | Every slide had been a grid of same-size cards of icon, heading and text. A different layout per slide: a statement beside a definition list, numbered rows, a loop beside a numbered list, two brand panels, a strip above a diagram, a Gantt chart, profiles. Icons removed from every item; the design floor added to the phase 4 checks. |
| A system drawn as bullets beside a small diagram. | A full-width diagram with a numbered process strip above it, step numbers as chips on the nodes, the boundary that matters as a dashed frame. |
| A registry, a host or an editor shown as a generic icon; use real logos and symbols, not an icon pack. | The real marks of every named product from their brand assets; the platform's wordmark and symbol; the client's logo. |
| A date wrapped inside a tile. | The tile row replaced by a definition list; `word-break: keep-all` so that no word breaks inside; wrapped values added to the review list. |
| Does the deck show that the work runs in a loop, on feedback? | The `loop` block: the repeating steps around a ring with the tools' marks at the center, beside the numbered steps. |

## Content

| The note | The move |
|---|---|
| The schedule carries a cadence and an interim date the outline never gave. | Only the outline's schedule, as a Gantt chart: a bar per phase, a diamond for the deadline. |
| A "things to decide today" slide and an appendix of figures. | Both cut. The figures the audience needs moved into the method slide. |
| The overview repeats the project's name, which everyone in the room knows; the out-of-scope list need not be spelled out. | Cut. The overview is the goal and the facts. |
| Two durations that are alternatives per item were presented as if they added up. | Stated as alternatives, with the rule that decides the total. |
| The method skips the preparation. | The preparation the outline implies (what is set up before the cycle runs) added as steps of the method. |
| The platform the work stands on is invisible. | A slide of its own: what it is, where it comes from, what it does here. |
| Items the outline gives equal time should have equal bars; the delivery period is work too. | Equal bars for the sequence, the delivery period as a bar, the deadline as a diamond. |
| Why does the quality bar talk about who signs off? | Cut. A quality bar is the floor and the open ceiling, two sentences. |
| Two products are confused, or one is shown where the other is used. | Told apart in the brand panel: which one the work uses, what it talks to, with each one's own mark. |
| A figure the room does not need (a period when the deadline is what matters). | The row cut; the figure that matters kept. |

## Wording

| The note | The move |
|---|---|
| The word chosen for a thing is awkward; a compound reads as foreign in the reviewer's language. | A plain word, with "to be confirmed" where that matters; the ordinary term, and the audience's ear decides. |
| The team slide wants a subtitle that names the team's specialty, and each person's title should name their role in that specialty. | A subtitle on the team slide; titles that name the role in this work, in the organization's own words for it. |

## What generalizes

- A note that names one slide usually names a default the whole deck repeats. Fix the default, then every slide.
- A note on the whole deck's look points at lazy composition, not at the tool. The answer is the design floor, not a different tool.
- Real marks beat icons every time a thing has a name. An icon is for the rare item whose meaning a word does not carry.
- A visual check is a check only when it is written down: a wrapped value, a word broken inside, a loop whose center touches its nodes are on the phase 4 list because each one has reached a reviewer.
- Facts the reviewer confirms (a name, a date, a title) are recorded where the organization keeps its records, and the deck file is the only thing edited.
