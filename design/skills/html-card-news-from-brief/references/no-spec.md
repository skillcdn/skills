# With no format spec: the stand-in

Read only when the user wants slides before any format, the routes to a spec (format-spec.md "Where a spec comes from") having been offered first. This is not a format; it is the least a carousel needs so that a user sees something before a format is made. Every checkpoint and the delivery say the post was built on this page and not on an account's format.

- Canvas: the platform's documented portrait size, looked up at run time (1080 by 1350 for 4:5 at the time of writing, illustrative).
- Two slide types. A cover: a full-bleed photo, a gradient over its lower part, a headline of two lines in a heavy free sans that covers the post's script, inside side margins of about a fifteenth of the width, its block ending about a ninth of the height above the bottom. A text slide: a full-bleed photo with the same gradient, a one-line heading in the heavy weight and up to three lines of body in the regular weight at about a forty-fifth of the height, bottom-left inside the same margins. A photo-only slide is the text slide with no text. A text slide whose beat has no legible image of what it names in the brief stands on the flat near-black instead of a photo, with the same blocks, rather than on a photo of another day or another subject.
- Fonts: one free sans for the post's script with a regular and a heavy weight, loaded from its distributor's documented path, named at the plan checkpoint.
- Colors: white text; a gradient from transparent to about 80% black where the type sits; a near-black flat (about #111) for a text slide without a photo.
- Density: a headline line of about twelve characters in a script of syllable blocks and about twenty-five in a Latin script; body lines of about thirty in a script of syllable blocks and about thirty-five in a Latin script, since a line of forty syllable blocks wraps in the stand-in's column at its body size; counted before setting, as script.md says.
- Register: the stand-in names none, so the slides take the voice the brief's `stance` and `tone` imply, said at the plan checkpoint, unless the user names one: a calm report in short polite sentences (in Korean the ~요 and ~세요 endings) with noun endings for labels; a side's case, a commentary or an aggressive tone in plain declaratives and sentence-final noun phrases (in Korean the ~다 ending and the noun ending), the headline a quoted charge or a statement with who made it in the sub. A polite voice on an aggressive brief read as soft, and the user asked for a harder one.

Nothing here is measured or designed; a spec replaces all of it.
