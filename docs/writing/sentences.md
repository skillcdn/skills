# Sentences, titles and what is cut

How a sentence is built so that a reader can use it, what a title and a heading are for, and what is cut from a draft. The limits of one medium (a slide's characters per line, a caption's length, an overview's sentences) are that medium's and are counted from its own spec.

## One idea per sentence

- One idea to a sentence, a row, a step or a cell. Where the material has more, split it.
- A full sentence where the text speaks, a short label where it names. A label followed by fragments is neither.
- An item of a numbered list of decisions or findings is a lead in bold and one plain sentence.
- A decision is written as what will be done, in one sentence, with who does it where one side does it.
- A note under a table or a figure ends in a noun or a verb stem ("the vendor's quote to be confirmed"; in Korean the noun form, 확인이 필요). Written as a full sentence with a period, it reads as the document's own opinion.

## Titles and headings

- A title names the content, not the writer's stance toward the reader.
- A heading says what the reader does with what is under it: "items to confirm", not "pending" or "under discussion".
- A label above a title, and a number on a section, stand only where the reader uses them to find something.

## What is cut

- A closing sentence that explains what the text meant. The last sentence is a fact, a noun phrase or the ask.
- A summary of what was just said, and a note at the foot of a page that restates the page.
- An overview that retells how things went. An overview says what was gone through, never how it went.
- A date that carries no information: the date beside an author's name, the date of a document that was only shown.
- A caveat said twice.
- In a record of what was decided, the intentions behind it and how it was reached.
- A text that is too long is cut, not shrunk: the section the reader did not need goes, and the type stays readable.

The full list of what makes a text read as machine-written, with the fix for each, is [`/docs/variation/not-ai.md`](/docs/variation/not-ai.md) "Signals in words": the same ending on every sentence, a triad, a rhetorical question that answers itself, a line that could be any subject's, a middle dot or a long dash inside a sentence.

## Rewriting

- A sentence that fails a check is rewritten, not softened.
- Read the finished text once more as the editor of the place it will appear would: a line that editor would not print is rewritten.
- A reviewer's note on one item usually names a default the whole text repeats. Fix the default, then every item.
