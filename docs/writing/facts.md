# Facts, figures and quotes in a text

What a text may state and how it states it: where its content comes from, how a figure is carried, what is said of a thing not yet known, when a hedge is right, how a quote and a claim are written, and how dates and names are checked. A text is exactly as firm as its source.

## Where the content comes from

- Content comes from the material the user gave, the sources the run read, and the user's answers. No figure, name, price or promise they do not contain.
- A source is read in full before it is used. A search summary, an excerpt in a search result or the agent's memory is a pointer, never a source.
- Where the sources are silent, the text stays silent or says what is to be confirmed. A thin source makes a shorter text, never an invented one.
- No proof the subject cannot supply: a customer's experience nobody had, a number its own page does not state.
- A version, a license or a package's name is read from its source, not recalled.
- What the source does not say is said to be not said, with what was looked for; what a source cannot show is stated as its limit. What comes from outside the source is marked as outside it.
- A fact only one source carries is kept and said to rest on one source.
- A source the run could not read is named, with why, beside what was read.
- The agent's own rendering of a source (a figure described in words, a scan transcribed) is said to be one, and a claim that rests on it names the place in the original.

## A figure

- A figure carries its source or its tag, where the medium keeps them: a row of a list, a tag beside the figure, a line under the table, the caption of a post. A figure without its source is a claim.
- A figure someone quoted aloud (a price, a size) is written with its caveat in the same sentence.
- A figure computed from the inputs shows its formula, and the delivery lists it as computed.
- Durations given as alternatives are stated as alternatives, with what decides the total; they are never summed.
- A count, not an impression: "5 of 8", not "most".
- A label is never stronger than the evidence. A class the numbers have not reached is not named; where the reader expects one, the first line says none was reached.
- A superlative or a comparison with no source ("the fastest", "the first") is dropped.

## To be confirmed

- What is not known yet is said to be confirmed, in the text's own words and language, and never guessed.
- In Korean the form follows the place: 확인 필요 as a tag on a figure, 확인사항 as the heading of the open items, 확인이 필요 at the end of a note.
- Something with no time in the material is an item to confirm, not a task with a time of the agent's; a time nobody set as a deadline is written as planned, not due.
- A finished text carries no placeholder. A draft the user completes (a line that needs a fact about their own product, a legal page that waits for its operator's name) carries a marked blank (`[owner]`), listed in the delivery as what is left to fill.
- The delivery lists what the user confirms before the text goes out: a figure tagged to be confirmed, a name or a title taken as spoken, a date the material left open.
- A fact the user or a reviewer confirms (a name, a title, a date) is recorded where the organization keeps its records, so that the next text starts from it.

## A hedge

A hedge belongs where the source is hedged, and nowhere else.

| The sentence is | It is written |
|---|---|
| A fact that has a source | Stated, with the source where the medium keeps sources. No "it seems" or "reportedly" |
| A decision | Firm: what will be done, and who does it where one side does it |
| An answer or a forecast someone gave | No firmer than what was said: "is expected to", not "will" |
| A reading of events that someone published | As that outlet's or that person's, quoted and attributed. The agent's own sense of why a story matters is not a reading to print |
| An analysis the text itself makes (a report's findings, a recommendation) | Named as the analysis, kept apart from the evidence it rests on, and counted, with the rules and thresholds it applied and the evidence against a finding beside the evidence for it |

## A quote and a claim

- A quote is written as its source printed it, marked as a quote, with who said it and in what capacity. It is never paraphrased into a fact.
- A claim keeps who made it ("the party's leader said"): it is a fact about what was said, not about the world.
- A rule about money, access or safety is quoted, never only paraphrased. A lead may say it in its own words, and the quote beneath carries the rule's own numbers, names and conditions.
- Two sources that disagree are both quoted, and the text says so. Which one it follows has an order and a written reason: an official release or document before a report of it, a passage about the case before a general rule. The agent does not choose between them without saying it did.
- A quote is read once more in its source before the text is sent.
- In a chat message that comes with a file the run wrote, a long quotation does not belong: point to the source and let the file carry the sentence. An answer given in the chat itself carries its quotes.

## Dates, names and titles

- Dates are absolute: a "yesterday" in an article dated the 22nd is written as the 21st. A weekday is computed from the date, never copied.
- An attendee the records do not confirm is not named, and a title is never guessed: the confirmed names with their titles, then "and others" in the text's language. A place takes its official name, from the records.
- A person in a published story is named as the source names them, with the capacity it gives.
- A name is taken from where it is written (a page, a frame, a sign), not from a transcript of speech.
- A reader's word is read as the source's term only where the source equates them, and the text says which reading it took.
- Two things easily confused (a tool and the service it runs on) are told apart: which one is meant, and what it talks to.
