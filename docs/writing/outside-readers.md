# A text that leaves the organization

What a document for a client, a partner or the public does not carry, where that goes instead, and how tools and other parties' words are named in it. Settled before the text is planned, because it decides what the text is made from.

## What stays inside

A document that goes outside carries nothing the organization said for its own ears:

- its costs, its staffing and its pricing logic;
- its judgments, its doubts, and its reasons for a figure;
- the terms of its own contracts, and terms it has not agreed to;
- a remark about the reader;
- anything that would read as a commitment the contract does not make.

The rule follows the reader. A document the organization's own people decide from (a plan for its board, a report for its investors) keeps such a line where its reader needs it, tagged for what it is: the use of funds, a role still to be hired, a figure still open.

## Where it goes instead

- What stays out is listed before the text is written, at the skill's first checkpoint, where the user sees it.
- A record sent to a client or a partner (the minutes of a meeting) also has it written where the organization keeps its own notes, in the same run.
- The delivery names what was left out on purpose.

## Naming tools and products

The two kinds of document differ, and each rule holds for its own:

| The text is | A tool is named |
|---|---|
| A record sent to the other party (the minutes of a meeting) | By the generic noun for the kind of thing it is ("the mail service", "the framework it is built on"), where it is the organization's own tooling; never the product's name, and never "the tool". The name stays in the organization's own record |
| A proposal that tells the other party what the work stands on | By its real name, on the page that is about the tools, with what it is and what it does in this work |

## Another party's words

- Another party's document text and its prices appear only where the material was supplied for that purpose.
- A published reading, a statement or a figure of someone else's is theirs in the text: quoted and attributed ([facts.md](facts.md) "A quote and a claim").
