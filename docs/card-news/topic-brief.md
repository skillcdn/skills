# The topic brief

The topic brief is one story made ready to be built: who it is for, the angle as a published interpretation, every fact with the sentence it comes from, where sources disagree, what could not be verified, and the images with their credits, sizes and licence terms. The research skill writes it as a JSON file (`topic-brief.json`) in a brief folder that also holds `img/` (the downloaded images kept), `img-unused/` (the downloads set aside, each with its reason in `credits.md`), `credits.md` (one line per kept image, then the set-aside list), `facts.md` (the facts table) and `candidates.md` (the list the user picked from); the build skill reads the JSON and `credits.md` and writes nothing on a slide that is not in them. The example JSON the research skill ships is the shape; this page says what each field means. A fact the build needs and the brief lacks goes back to the research, never onto a slide from memory.

## Fields

| Field | Holds | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `audience` | `scope` (domestic or global), `market` (the country or region the readers are in), `language` (the post's language) and `readers` (who the post is for, what they know of the subject and what of theirs the story touches, in the user's words), which the angle, the facts and the beats are chosen for, a fact in the readers' own case before a general figure. | The user, asked once: the readers always, with the request's implication as the default; the market and the language for the half the request leaves open. |
| `stance`, `tone` | `stance`: `report`, `commentary`, or `advocacy` with `side` (whose case the post makes); `tone`: `calm` or `aggressive`. The stance orders the readings, facts and quotes and places the other side's reply; the tone sets how far the headline and the subs go within the facts and the attributed quotes. | The user, asked once with the readers; the request's implication as the default. |
| `rights` | How the images' terms are said: `flag-all`, `flag-forbidden` or `list-once`. Every entry keeps its `licence` whatever the value. | The user, asked once with the readers. |
| `topic` | The story in one line, as the user picked it, and the day of the pick. | The candidate list checkpoint, or the request. |
| `canvas` | The slide size the images are judged against: `platform`, `size` (width and height), `source` (the format spec, or the platform's help page) and `looked_up`. With a spec it repeats the spec's size; without one it is the size the build renders at. | The spec, else the platform's documented portrait size looked up at run time. |
| `angle` | The interpretation the post runs on, as one sentence in the agent's words, with the outlet, the date, the URL and the quoted sentence of the article that states it, and the other articles that share it; `alternatives` holds a second published reading with its own sources. An angle without a quoted sentence is not an angle; a story with none yet says so and the post stays a report. | Two or more articles that frame the story the same way; the one that states it best is quoted. |
| `headline` | A working headline in the angle's words, or from the leading fact when there is no angle; a starting point for the build. | The research skill. |
| `facts` | One entry per fact: `id`, the fact in one sentence in the post's language, `kind` (number, date, place, quote, event, claim), `source` (outlet, date, URL, the article's own sentence, and `kind`: `article`, or `official` for an organizer's or a venue's own page, which sources a schedule, a price, an address or an opening and nothing else), `also_in` (the other articles that carry it), `single_source` (true when no other article read carries it), `note` (optional: a caveat the build keeps, such as a third party to name with a figure, or `organizer's release reprinted` when the article reprints a release). | The article bodies, read in full. |
| `disagreements` | A fact that two articles give differently: both values, both sources, which the brief uses and the reason. | The cross-check. |
| `unverified` | What the post would like to say and no article confirmed: an account handle for a mention, an official notice, a figure found only in a search summary; each with where it was looked for. Listed so that the user checks before publishing. | The gaps the research met. |
| `images` | One entry per image: `file`; `source_kind` (article-figure, share-image, video-frame, subject-page, page-capture, image-search, user); `shows`, what the image shows in the post's language, so that a slide's text is matched to an image of what it names; `caption` as the article printed it or as the text printed on a frame; `credit` (agency or broadcaster and program, photographer where named, the outlet or channel it appeared in, the date); `url` (a frame's with its time); `px` (width, height; for a frame, the resolution the clip was served at, which the capture table records, not the file's size); `legibility` at phone size (clear, soft, unreadable); `watermark` (where a logo, a label or a credit mark sits); `licence` (the terms the publisher states; `modification_allowed` true, false or unknown); `role` (cover, subject, scene, mood, detail, illustration); `notes` (a horizontal image that loses a subject when cropped to portrait; a share image credited to its outlet; the footage's origin a clip names; a re-enactment or a generated image inside the material). `images_searched_for` lists the beats that needed an image search and what was looked for first. | Where the articles point, in order: their figures and captions, the clip or footage or post they cite as frames with time codes, the subject's public pages, an image search last; the files on disk. |
| `candidates` | `shown`, the list at the checkpoint, each with its one-line angle, why it is timely, the articles that carry it, its rank and whether it was the recommended one; and `excluded`, what the account had already covered, or none. | The candidate phase. |
| `beats` | A suggested order of five to eight beats, each naming the fact ids it would carry and an image role, as a starting point for the build. Not the script. | The research skill's reading of the story. |
| `articles_read` | Every article read in full: outlet, author where named, date and time, URL, and its kind (report, analysis, interview). | The reading. |

## How the build reads it

Every sentence the build writes names the fact ids it rests on; an element's lines name the image caption or the quoted source sentence they come from; the headline rests on `angle` or `headline`; the caption's first paragraph carries the facts with their dates and the outlets, and its credit line is `credits.md`, rewritten in the spec's pattern when the two differ. Mentions come only from a fact that carries a handle with its source; anything in `unverified` goes to the pre-publish checklist. An image whose `licence.modification_allowed` is false is used whole, with no crop, dim or overlay, or not at all; one marked unknown is used as the account's slide type treats its images, with its credit, and the delivery says so. A slide carries an image whose `shows` names what the slide's text names; an image marked `unreadable` goes on no slide that has to be read, a `soft` one is said so at the build's checkpoint, and a frame's own `caption` is what a slide of a type that keeps frames whole says, so such a slide takes only a frame whose caption states a fact of the table, named in the frame's `shows`; a frame whose caption no fact carries is material for what it shows, not for what it says. `beats` is the starting order of the build's plan; where they outnumber the build's slides, the build merges or moves them and says so. With no spec, `canvas` is the slide size the build renders at.
