# The comparison sheet: how candidates are shown and chosen

The user chooses from one sheet, and the sheet is made so that the choice is between moves and not between impressions: positions on the axes, the mechanism each keeps, what each changes, its verdict, its risk, and what it would take to produce, with a recommendation and a runner-up. A concept's sheet is a table with each candidate's opening lines under it; a look's sheet is a table beside a contact sheet of one representative screen per candidate. Four to six candidates, never more.

## The columns

| Column | What goes in |
|---|---|
| Name | One word or two that say the move ("the customer's voice", "one color"), never a number alone and never an adjective of quality. |
| Position | One line per axis moved, in the words of axes.md and, where the medium has them, of its producing skill (a slide type, a block, a mark); "holds" for the rest. |
| Mechanism | How it gets the reference's effect: the mechanism line with this candidate's device and figure in it. |
| Keeps and changes | What stays from the reference's mechanism; what the viewer would see or hear differently at a glance. |
| Verdict | "Passed" with the fixes made, in a few words, or the one signal kept and its reason; a candidate that did not pass is not on the sheet. |
| Risk | What could make it fail with this audience: a prior knowledge they may lack, a proof the maker must supply, a register the brand has not used. |
| To produce | In the medium's own units (shots and takes, slides, pages, a theme and its blocks) where the medium is known; never a figure of money or credits, which the producing skill estimates at its own checkpoint. |
| The sample | A concept: its opening lines and its signature line, as they would be spoken or read. A look: the representative screen, named, on the contact sheet. |

## The recommendation and the runner-up

The recommendation is one candidate, argued in the effect brief's terms: which reading it serves best, what prior knowledge it rests on that the audience has, and what it asks of the producer beyond the near candidate. The runner-up is named with what it would give instead, in one line. The far candidate stays on the sheet with one line on why it is there even when it is not recommended: it is where the next request can go. A recommendation without a runner-up, or a sheet without the far candidate, is not shown.

## The contact sheet

For looks: every candidate's representative screen at the same size on one image, in the sheet's order, the name under each, the near one first and the far one last; for a page, the phone screens beside the sheet, one per candidate, because the phone width is where the verdict was made. The reference is not on the sheet, so that the comparison is among the candidates; it is linked beside it. The screen is the unit the audience meets first (a cover, a first slide, a first screen of a page, a frame), made by the cheapest means of the medium; for a medium rendered in a browser, a page written for it and screenshotted, each screen checked at the size its reader will see it. A screen that could not be rendered is shown as its position in words, said so, and never as a stand-in from another medium.

## What the user can say

- **Pick one.** The pick goes to the handoff as it is.
- **Merge two.** A merge is a new candidate: it is placed on the axes, judged by not-ai.md, and shown once more before it is handed off.
- **More on this axis.** Two or three new candidates that move on the named axis from the pick's position; the sheet stays within six, the weakest kept ones dropped, and is shown once more.
- **None.** The reference is read again for the reading the user disputes, and the sheet is remade from that brief.

## What is never shown

More than six candidates; a candidate with a known AI-feel failure; candidates that differ in words only; a recommendation argued by taste ("stronger", "cleaner") rather than by a reading; a figure of money or credits; the reference's own lines or screens as a candidate.
