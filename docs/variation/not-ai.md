# The AI feel: signals and the fix

"It sounds like AI" and "it looks like AI" are what a reader notices before anything else, and what a variation is sent back for first. The feel is not one thing: it is a bundle of defaults a model falls into when nothing decides otherwise, in words, in stories and in layouts. This page lists the signals by medium, the bundles that are the tell, the fix for each, and the positive tests a candidate must pass. Every candidate passes it before it is shown; one that fails is fixed or dropped, never shown with a caveat.

## Signals in words

| Signal | The fix |
|---|---|
| An explanatory closer: the last sentence says what the piece meant ("this is the story they are watching", "that is what makes it special"). | Cut it. The last line is a fact, a noun phrase or the ask. |
| A hedge where a fact has a source ("it seems", "reportedly", "arguably"). | State it, and put the source where the medium keeps sources. |
| Every sentence ends the same way; every line the same length; the same connective between every two facts. | Vary the endings at the proportion the reference's register shows, counted on the reference; let one line be short. |
| A symmetrical triad ("fast, simple, powerful"), a rhetorical question that answers itself, a colon and a list where a sentence would do. | One concrete claim, or the list as the medium's list type. |
| Abstractions standing in for things ("innovation", "seamless", "elevate", "unlock", "journey", "empower"). | The thing: what it does, in the words the product's own page uses. |
| First person where the voice is the brand's or the account's and no figure speaks; the agent's voice where the figure should speak. A figure who plays themselves speaks in the first person and passes. | The figure's voice, in the register's level. |
| A line that is the reference's with the words swapped. | A new line from the product's own world; the hook and the closing line first. |
| A line that could be any product's: take the product away and it still works. | A rule the product's own world gives; another device. |
| A hook that needs decoding (a brand term, a coined word, a name that carries nothing yet). | Every word one the viewer knew before; the figure as a title that says their power, the name on the end card. |
| The summary of what was just said, on the slide after it was said. | Cut it; the detail goes where the medium keeps detail (a caption, a note, the meeting). |

## Signals in a story or a concept

| Signal | The fix |
|---|---|
| A trope where a rule should be: the story runs on a stock situation and no "In this world, ..." sentence can be written for it. | Write the rule from the product's own world, and the device that carries it. |
| The reveal in the hook, or a later line that repeats the hook. | The hook names the anomaly, never its meaning; nothing is explained after the reveal. |
| The figure introduced by name and role instead of by what they do. | The figure enters as what they are, shown by one act. |
| Stakes the viewer does not have (a detail of the maker's craft, a setting only the maker knows). | A stake everyone has: money, time, the body, food, a child, a debt, a vow, a taboo. |
| A proof the product cannot supply (a customer's experience nobody had, a number its page does not state). | The product's own proof, or another proof axis; never an invented one. |
| Every candidate differs in words only: the same rule, figure and device under five hooks. | Candidates on different axes (axes.md); five hooks are one candidate's drafts. |

## Signals in a look

| Signal | The fix |
|---|---|
| Same-size cards of icon, heading and text as the structure; nested cards; a card per item. | Structure from type, rules and real marks: a statement beside a list, numbered rows, one dominant and the rest small. |
| Big-number tiles with small labels; a kicker or label above every heading; section numbers where the order carries nothing. | Figures in a definition list or inline; the heading carries its own weight. |
| A gradient of purple to blue on white; gradient text; decorative blur; a thick colored left border; a glow. | One accent on tinted neutrals, or the material's own colors; flat surfaces. |
| Emoji or glyphs as icons; a generic icon for a named thing; one icon per item. | A named thing by its real mark; an icon only for the rare item a word does not carry; most items with none. |
| Every slide or page the same layout; everything centered; the hero, three features and a call to action on every page. | No two consecutive units share a layout; the eye enters at one place; a layout chosen for what the unit says. |
| A stock photograph of hands on a laptop, a diverse team laughing, a city at night; a device mockup at an angle. | The product itself, the material the story has, a captured frame kept whole, type alone. |
| Hierarchy by size alone, or no hierarchy; type that is even everywhere. | Weight, width, case and position doing the work; a scale of a few steps. |
| A slide type, a block or a layout the reference account or the organization never uses, in a post built in its format or a slight change of it. | Only the types the spec has; a type seen once is tentative. A format of the user's own, or something new from a reference, makes its types at the user's pick. |
| A line broken inside a word; a lone word on a second line; a value that wraps in a tile. | Breaks at spaces placed by hand; fewer words; a definition list instead of the tile. |

## The bundle is the tell

One signal is a choice; three together are the default. A triad in the headline over a purple gradient above three icon cards; a kicker, a big number and a glow; a rhetorical question, a hedge and an explanatory closer. A candidate that carries one signal with a reason the sheet can state may keep it, and the sheet's verdict column states the reason; one that carries a bundle is rebuilt from its axes, not patched.

## The positive tests

A candidate passes these in addition to carrying no bundle:

- **The thumb test.** Read or look as a stranger scrolling past who has never heard of the product and will not read a caption: does it stop them before they know what any word means or who any figure is?
- **Take the product away.** If the concept still works, it is not this product's; if the look could sit on any product's page, it is not this product's.
- **A person chose this.** Every choice on the sheet has a reason a person could have had; "it looks modern" is not one, "one accent because the material's own colors carry the rest" is.
- **The reference's followers would not name it.** A reader who knows the reference recognizes the genre and not the piece.
- **One of these is not like the others** (on the set as a whole). On the sheet, the far candidate is far; if a stranger could not say which one it is, the candidates moved on words.

## Where each medium's own list lives

The sharper list for a medium stays with its skill and wins there: the register checks of the card news build (its script page), the design floor of the deck skill, the hook table and the thumb test of the ad skill. A producing skill links this page from its review and adds nothing of its own to it; a signal that turns out to be true of every medium moves here, in the sentence it bears on.
