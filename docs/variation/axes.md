# Axes: where a variation can move

A candidate is a position on a few axes, written down, so that the comparison sheet compares moves and not impressions and the user can ask for "one more on this axis". The axes below are the ones a run has needed; a medium may add one, named in the skill. A candidate moves on two or three axes and holds the rest at the reference's position: one that moves on every axis is a different piece and cannot be compared, one that moves on none is the reference.

## Concept axes

For a direction: a story, a post, a campaign, a page's argument, a deck's line.

| Axis | Positions, from the reference outward | What moving it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Stake | What the viewer already values or fears: money, time, the body, food, a child, a debt, a vow, status, belonging, safety, a taboo. | Whether a stranger stops. A detail of the maker's craft carries no stake until the story gives it one. |
| Figure | Who holds authority and speaks: the maker's own persona, an elder, an expert, a customer, nobody (a narrator over pictures), the viewer themselves. | Whose word the claim rests on, and what the product inherits at the end. |
| Device | The one concrete thing that carries the rule: an object, a number, a habit, a gesture, a date, a door. | What the viewer remembers. Another device carrying the same rule is a variation; the reference's device is the reference. |
| Form | On-camera dialogue, a narrator over pictures, a testimonial, a demo, a list, a story, text only, a conversation, a document. | How it is made and how it is paced. |
| Genre and register | The kind of story and its conventions (a folk tale, a deadpan joke, a confession, a how-to, breaking news, a letter), the speech level, the words it never uses. | The audience that recognizes it, and the prior knowledge it leans on. |
| Order of telling | What is withheld until when: the hook, the rule, the exception, the question, the answer, the reveal, the ask. | Where the turn lands and whether the reveal is felt. |
| Setting | The world, its time and its scale: a kitchen, a court, a street, a screen, a century ago, tomorrow. | The images available and the norms the viewer brings. |
| Proof | What makes the claim credible: a demonstration, a number, a named person, a before and after, an absence (what is not said), the maker's own record. | What the maker can honestly supply: a proof the product cannot carry is dropped, not faked. |

## Design axes

For a look: a slide, a post, a page, a cover, a frame.

| Axis | Positions, from the reference outward | What moving it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | One accent on tinted neutrals; a saturated field with ink on it; one color and black; the material's own colors with none added; a brand's pair. | Warmth, loudness, what reads as the maker's. |
| Type | One family on a small scale; a display face for the headline and a text face below; weight as the only hierarchy; width (condensed, wide); case; a script's own faces. | The voice of the page before a word is read. |
| Composition | Where the eye enters and the density: a grid of equals, one dominant and the rest small, a column with wide margins, edge to edge, stacked bands, a single centered object. | Whether the piece reads as one thing or many; what a phone shows at a third of the width. |
| Imagery | A photograph cropped full-bleed; a captured frame kept whole with its marks; an illustration; type alone; a diagram; a screenshot; the product itself; nothing. | What the piece shows and what it asks the viewer to imagine. |
| Shape and surface | One radius everywhere or none; borders or no borders; flat or shadowed; cards or no cards; rules and type instead of boxes. | How much the structure is drawn and how much is set. |
| Rhythm | Static; one motion that settles; a cut on every line; a loop; a pause where the reference has none. | Tempo, and what reads as alive against what reads as busy. |
| Tone of the copy on it | Two or three words for the voice, and what the copy never does. | Whether the look and the words agree. |
| Density, for a format | The start's counts; fewer sentences per slide with the rest in the caption; shorter lines; one fact per slide over more slides. | How much a reader takes in at a glance. Moved only when the request asks for less or more text: a measured format's density is what it is until the user says otherwise. |

## The near and the far candidate

- **The start:** the positions every candidate holds unless it moves them, written as the first row: the reference's; or, for a look with no reference, the existing design's, else the crowd's where the request wants the genre, else the skeleton's own.
- **Near:** one decision away from the start. For a concept, the device alone moved to another that carries the same rule, every other position held: the reference's device is the reference's, so the device moves in every candidate, and the near one moves nothing else. For a look, one design axis moved. It is the control: what the start would be with one decision changed, and the measure of how far the others went. A sheet without it cannot say whether the far ones are better or only different.
- **Far:** leaves two or three of the crowd's positions, the positions every piece of the subject's category shares (the same opening, the same proof shot, the same offer line, the same purple gradient), which the effect brief records from one category search, and holds the mechanism line; it may touch more axes than the others, and is the other exception to two or three. It is the open angle, kept on the sheet even when it is not recommended.
- **Between:** two to four candidates that move on two or three axes each, no two on the same set, so that the user's pick can be read as a preference for an axis.
- **The distance the request asks for:** a request for a slight change keeps every candidate within one or two axes of the start, the near one on one, and has no far one; one that names the axis and nothing else (warmer colors, nothing more) puts all four on that axis; a request for something new from the reference has no near one, every candidate moving two or three axes from the start and holding the mechanism line, the one on two axes being the control and the far one leaving the crowd's positions too; with neither, one near, one far and the rest between.

## Writing a position

One line per axis, in the sheet's own column, naming the position and not the adjective: "Figure: the customer, speaking to camera" and not "more relatable"; "Palette: one color and black" and not "bolder". A position the medium's producing skill cannot take (a slide type outside the start's format where the request asks for a slight change, a block the deck's floor rejects, a proof the product cannot supply) is not a position, and the candidate is moved or dropped. Where the medium and its producing skill are known and that skill carries the vocabulary for an axis (the deck skill's blocks, the card news spec's slide types, the ad skill's marks), the position is written in that vocabulary, so that the handoff needs no translation; otherwise in plain words.

## What an axis costs

An axis that changes words costs nothing to show. An axis that changes a look costs one representative screen per candidate, by the cheapest means of the medium: a page or a slide written as HTML is rendered in a browser; a still that must be generated is the family's own matter, with its estimate and its consent, and a design skill that cannot render it shows the position as words and says so. The sheet says for each candidate what it would take to produce in the medium's own units (shots, slides, pages) where the medium is known, never a figure of money or credits, which the producing skill estimates at its own checkpoint.
