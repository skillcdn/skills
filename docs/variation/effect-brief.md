# The effect brief: what a reference does, before what it looks like

A reference (an ad, a post, a deck, a page, a scene) is read for its effect before its surface. A variation written from the surface keeps the surface and changes the words, which is what a reader recognizes as a copy; a variation written from the effect keeps the mechanism and is free to change everything the viewer sees. The brief is one page: six readings, each one sentence and the device that carries it, a mechanism line, what stays and what is free, and three to five other references that get the same effect by other means. Every candidate is argued from it, and the comparison sheet reads it back.

## The six readings

| Reading | The question | Written as | Evidence, by medium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intent | What whoever had the piece made wanted the viewer to do or feel: the job of the piece. | One sentence, from that seat: "They wanted a stranger to ..." | An ad: the call to action and the offer. A post: the caption's close, what the account posts around it. A deck: the asks on the last slide, the audience. A page: the one action above the fold. |
| Effect | What happens to the viewer, and where in the piece: stop, read on, laugh, wince, trust, save, forward, nod. | One sentence per moment, in order, with the place: "At the second line the viewer ..." | An ad: the hook window, the turn, the reveal. A post: the cover, the slide where the detail lands, the last slide. A deck: the slide the room leans toward. A page: the first screen, the proof. |
| Aesthetics | The look and the register as choices, and what each signals: palette, type, density, rhythm, voice, speech level. | One sentence per choice with its signal: "A single accent on tinted neutrals says ..." | What is on screen, measured where it can be (sizes, counts, the number of colors, lines per slide, words per line) and named where it cannot. |
| Fun | The pleasure the piece gives and the device that gives it: a joke, a surprise, a pattern completed, a taboo brushed, a competence shown, a secret shared, a thing named exactly. | The device, concrete: "The fun is the one odd act a formidable figure does for one person." | The line or the frame the viewer would quote or screenshot; where the viewer's face changes. |
| Expected effect | What the maker measures or hopes for after the viewing: a click, a share, a save, a sign-up, a reply, a yes in the room. | One sentence: "They expect ..." | The offer, the handle, the link, the ask; what the piece makes easy to do next. |
| Prior knowledge | What the viewer must already know for the piece to work: a genre, a norm, a meme, a product category, a figure's kind, a season. | A list, each item with whether the directions' own audience has it; what the reference's viewers had and this audience lacks is named, so that the mechanism line is rewritten on knowledge they have. | Every word and image the piece does not explain. A hook that needs decoding fails here first. |

Read in that order and write each as one entry: one sentence, or one sentence per moment or per choice where the reading has several, with its evidence beside it; a reading that cannot be written that way has not been found yet. Where a reading is measured (the hook window in seconds, the lines per slide, the colors), the number is in the sentence.

## The mechanism line

One sentence that joins the readings: "It works because <the effect> comes from <the device>, which rests on <the prior knowledge>, and the viewer is expected to <the expected effect>." The line is the thing a variation keeps. A reference whose mechanism line could be written about any piece of its genre has been read for its genre, not for itself: read again for the device. Where the piece tells a story, write beside the line the rule its world runs on, "In this world, ...", which the device carries; a candidate writes its own rule. The device is the one concrete thing (an object, a number, a habit, an act, a line) and never the piece's premise (a confession, a refusal, a tour), which is its genre and its order of telling: the device is never taken, the premise is a position that holds or moves.

## What stays and what is free

- **Stays:** the mechanism line; the medium and the length, unless the request changes them; the register's level (deadpan stays deadpan, a legend stays a legend) unless an axis moves it on purpose; the place of the turn in the order of telling.
- **Free:** the situation, the setting, the cast, every line, the palette, the type, the composition, the imagery, the device itself where another device carries the same rule.
- **Never taken:** the reference's words, its people, its marks, its layout as a layout, its device as the device. A line that is the reference's with the words swapped is the reference's.
- An axis moved on purpose overrides "stays" for that candidate; "stays" is the position every candidate starts from.

## Other references with the same effect

Before candidates, find three to five other pieces that get the same effect by other means. Search by the Effect reading and the device, in a short phrase each, not by the subject's category: "a figure's one exception" and not the product's category; "a number that reframes a habit" and not the brand. Run the category search once all the same, and record what every result of it shares (the opening, the proof, the offer line, the look) as the crowd: the positions the far candidate leaves. Where the pack has a research skill for the medium (running ads, an account's posts, pages on the open web), it does the finding and its method is its own; where it does not, the open web is read. For each, write in one line what it keeps of the mechanism and what it does differently to the same end: the different thing is a candidate's seed. A reference found this way is read, cited by where it was seen, and never reused; the brief records it so that the sheet can say where a candidate's move came from.

## What a brief never holds

The reference's text beyond the quotable line that is the evidence of a reading; a real person's identity; a candidate. A brief that proposes is a brief that has stopped reading.
