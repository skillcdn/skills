# Pronunciation: what the video models do with sounds

Video models with native dialogue have no pronunciation dictionary, and a transcript often cannot show the fault. This file is the knowledge, per language, gathered from real runs of every Higgsfield skill of the repository; the procedure that uses it (how lines are written, how a take is read, how a retry is made) is each skill's own. A fault kept out of the first take costs nothing; one found in a take costs a retry; one found by the user costs their trust.

This is the one copy. A new outcome goes into the list it belongs to, with the word as a sound example, what the decodes wrote and what settled it; an agent running a skill without the repository at hand reports it in its delivery instead. Add a language section when a run teaches one.

## In any language

- **Spell for the ear in the prompt, from the first take.** A model reads letters. A name or a key word that is not said the way it is written goes into the take prompt the way it sounds, in the language's own script, while the intended line and the subtitle keep the spelling; an ordinary word stays as written (below). This is the dictionary trick of text-to-speech tools; it has fixed every fault of the kinds listed below as fixed by spelling.
- **The first word of a take is said fastest.** Never a name there; give it a lead-in word, and ask for about half a second of silence before the first word: a take can start mid-word and lose its first consonant, which no edit restores, and a name said first was sung too fast for its vowel to be certain on two takes, with a retry that asked for it clearly changing nothing.
- **Pace is written in numbers.** A line prompted with its rate (about seven syllables a second, pauses under half a second, half a second of lead) comes out at that rate with every syllable present; a line prompted by mood ("unhurried", "weighty") came out a third slower, with pauses of a second and a half, and dragged even after the pauses were cut.
- **An ordinary word that failed is said another way in the retry**, with a plainer word of the same meaning; the intended line and the subtitle change with it. A name or a key word is respelled, then its syllables are named after the line, then it goes on screen as text; it is never changed without the user.
- **Names and loanwords in another script** are written in the prompt the way the dialogue language says them (a Latin name in Hangul for Korean); the subtitle keeps the original spelling.
- **Only names and key words are kept clear of the sounds a model slurs.** Most words with those sounds come out right; a script rewritten around sounds loses its voice. Ordinary words are left as written and the review catches the rest.

## Korean

All of it is Seedance 2.5 speaking Korean at five to seven syllables a second (checked 2026-10); a new outcome moves a sound from one list to another.

- **Fixed by spelling for the ear**, so spelled that way from the first take: an aspirated cluster ("적혔다" as "저켰다": two takes came out without the aspiration and were written "적겼다" at about 0.85, which was the truth and not a homophone; the respelling gave "적혔다" at 0.98, with about 50 milliseconds of aspiration noise at the onset against 10 to 20 before; "축하해" as "추카해", "약혼자와" as "야콘자와"); a final consonant carried onto a particle ("…님의" as "…니메" and "…님은" as "…니믄", after the ㅁ was lost in three places of one take; "크레딧이" as "크레디시", "갚아" as "가파", "쪽은" as "쪼근"); a double final ("까닭" as "까닥", the standard pronunciation, after it came out as "가도" and took the hook's meaning with it); an assimilated or tensed cluster ("식만" as "싱만", "첫날밤부터" as "천날빰부터"). Written this way from the first take, none of these words has failed; six in one episode all came out right (the small decode wrote "첫날반부터" for the last, the two larger ones the intended word).
- **Fixed by naming the syllables in Latin letters after the line**, in a key word, the line itself unchanged: ㅟ heard as ㅚ by the medium and the largest decode at 0.82 to 0.94 while the small heard it right ("취소" as "최소"; "chwi and so, a clear wi" made all three write it right at 0.81 to 0.88); ㅡ heard as ㅜ by both plain decodes ("증인" as "중인", "중위"; "jeung-in, an unrounded eu" made the medium and the largest write it right at 0.81 and 0.91 while the small wrote "징인" at 0.55, and two of three settled it); a plain ㅈ inside a word at about 6.4 syllables a second ("소재" as "소회"; "so and jae, with a clear j sound" in a pickup at about six a second fixed it).
- **Slurred, and no spelling helped**: 져 and 겨 inside a word, heard as 서 and 려 ("뒤져서" as "뒤서서"; respelled "뒤저서" changed nothing, and a pickup with another verb in its place came out clean in both decodes at 0.99); "견적" (heard as "견약"; "견적뿌터" changed nothing, and the word was cut out of the audio and put on a card); the counter "권" after a number at about six syllables a second (heard as "간" at about 0.68 in an 18-second take and as "건" in a 4-second pickup whose prompt respelled the phrase; a prompted decode read "권" at 0.41 and settled nothing, so the delivery asked the user to listen). An ordinary word with these sounds that failed is said another way in the retry.
- **Smeared, replaced before the first take**: a noun ending in a consonant cluster before a vowel-initial particle ("진흙이나", "진흙에"), smeared into other syllables in several takes and both decodes until a plain synonym ("진창이나") or a consonant-initial continuation ("진흙 속에") replaced it; clusters before a consonant ("진흙발") are fine. A chain of Sino-Korean syllables or a coined term, unrecognized by both decodes; use a plain phrase of the same meaning.
- **A syllable added**: the subject particle "이" after a one-syllable noun ("돌이") came back doubled ("돌이가") until another particle was used ("돌은") with "no added syllables" in the prompt.
- **A name's vowel bent**: a two-syllable name with ㅓ was said with ㅗ even with a Latin respelling in the prompt; a name is what the viewer learns first, so leave it out of the line when the sentence survives, and never put it first.
- **The first word lost**: a voice-over take started mid-word at frame 0 and lost its first consonant ("묻는" heard as "웃는"); the retry, prompted for about half a second of silence before the line, came with a 0.47-second lead and the exact words.
- **Pace in numbers held**: a 23-second voice-over of 160 syllables prompted at about 7 per second with pauses under half a second came out at 7.27 per second with every syllable present.
- **Written differently, heard the same**, so no fault: ㅐ and ㅔ ("세로", "새로"), a loanword's vowel or final ("숏폼", "쇼폼"), a numeral for its word, spacing.
- **One decoder's ear, settled by the others**: a verb ending ("피운" written "피우는" by one decode), a plain initial written tense by one decode ("서 줘" as "써 줘") while two plain decodes agreed on the intended word; a whispered line that the medium model did not decode at all while the small and the largest did; a forced laugh right before a line ("좋아요") that dropped every decode's probabilities under 0.1 while the envelope showed the speech and the words were right.
